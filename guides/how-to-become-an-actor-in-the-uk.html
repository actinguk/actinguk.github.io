How to become an actor in the UK

A practical route from zero credits to your first professional opportunities.

Acting UK editorialPublished 13 November 2026

Five practical steps

  1. Start with the craft. Take an affordable class, join a local theatre group or make a short scene with collaborators. Drama school is one route, not a universal requirement.
  2. Create an honest one-page CV, a recent headshot and a short clip showing your work. List real training, credits and skills; do not invent experience.
  3. Build experience through carefully vetted student films, fringe theatre and open calls. Understand pay, travel, footage rights and who is producing before accepting.
  4. Research casting platforms and their eligibility. Identify suitable agents only when you have materials that show what you can do.
  5. Keep a weekly rhythm: practise, make work, apply selectively, review your progress and protect your income.

Build a route that fits your circumstances

There is no single path. Compare formal training with part-time classes and practical experience. If you are changing careers, start by establishing a weekly training routine and a realistic budget. Do not treat a training course as a guarantee of employment.

Your first three months

  • Month one: choose a class or theatre group, practise a scene and write an honest CV.
  • Month two: build a small portfolio, research open calls and record a self-tape.
  • Month three: review your materials, seek feedback and make targeted applications.

Professional casting and representation

Spotlight has eligibility criteria; a drama-school degree is not the only route. Read its current joining requirements. An agent may help later, but you can build relevant skills and experience first. See our agency directory.
