How do you get a London acting agent if you have no connections?

— Look for a professional fit, not the biggest name. The best first agency is one that understands your work and has room for you.

Acting UK editorialPublished 13 November 2026

Make a shortlist from the agency's real client roster and submission policy. Look for evidence of work you would like to do and ask what the agency offers actors at your stage. Do not assume that an agency is taking new clients.

Write a short, personal introduction with your location, work eligibility, training and relevant credits. Include one strong headshot, your CV and a link to a short reel or scene. Give a concrete reason you are approaching that agency.

Ask about commission, expenses, exclusivity, payment handling and termination before signing. Follow up politely only if the agency's guidance permits it; send genuinely new work rather than repeated identical messages. Avoid promises of guaranteed jobs or pressure to purchase a package.

London-specific networking

Use screenings, theatre events and workshops to develop genuine professional relationships. Follow a conversation with a relevant, brief introduction rather than treating networking as an obligation to offer representation.
