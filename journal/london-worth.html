Is it still worth moving to London to become an actor?

— London is useful, not compulsory. The right question is whether living there gives you enough opportunity to justify its cost.

Acting UK editorialPublished 13 November 2026

London offers theatre, screen networks, training and industry events. Yet many first-round auditions are remote, and productions work across the UK. A lower-cost base with reliable transport and a good self-tape setup may give you more time to practise than a costly London room.

Compare two realistic plans: your current base with occasional London trips, and a London house share with a flexible job. Include travel, time, accommodation, training and lost income. Look at the kind of acting you want, not just the number of theatres nearby.

If you can test the city first, do so. Arrange classes or meetings, try actual commutes and check rental listings. Move when the benefits are concrete and you have a financial buffer, not because you feel behind everyone else.
