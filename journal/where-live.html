Where should actors live in London?

— Choose a room around your work, transport and daily life, rather than the prestige of a postcode.

Acting UK editorialPublished 13 November 2026

Start with a map of the places you actually need: job, classes, regular rehearsal spaces and a station with useful connections. Compare journey times at night and at weekends, not just during the morning peak.

As search starting points, look along well-connected routes in north, east, south and west London, and compare outer areas with central rooms. Finsbury Park, Tottenham, Walthamstow, Leyton, Stratford, Lewisham and Acton are examples to investigate, not a ranked list of cheap neighbourhoods.

At a viewing, check the room's noise, daylight, heating, broadband, space to film a self-tape and your housemates' schedules. A slightly more expensive room that lets you work and record reliably can be better value than a cheaper one with a long commute.
