Attention: the art of listening when the script can change

Jules Head and Georgia Bruce bring their formally adventurous two-hander to the Bush Studio, with its original Dublin cast.

Acting UK editorial 15th November 2026

Most actors spend rehearsals learning when to speak. In Attention, the more interesting question may be when to stop. The play, co-created by director Jules Head and writer Georgia Bruce, arrives at the Bush Theatre’s Studio on 19th November after its debut at Dublin Fringe Festival in 2025.

Bláithín Mac Gabhann and Norah Lopez Holden return to the roles they originated. The piece examines a queer relationship built over seven years, but its form is as important as its subject. The performers can interrupt the telling, move backwards or forwards, or abandon a passage altogether. The venue describes the choices as part of each evening’s performance, rather than a fixed sequence of theatrical effects.

For actors, this is a particularly intriguing proposition. A conventional two-hander already requires an acute sense of the other performer’s timing. Here, listening appears to have consequences for the architecture of the evening itself. The challenge is not simply to make a familiar exchange sound spontaneous, but to negotiate a shared account of events while remaining responsive to a partner. It is a useful distinction for anyone working with devised material or improvisation.

The Dublin production received seven nominations at the festival’s awards, including Best Production. The London engagement runs until 23rd December, with tickets listed at £15 to £25. The Bush also lists captioned and audio-described performances in December. This is a preview of the forthcoming London run, rather than a verdict on its performances.
