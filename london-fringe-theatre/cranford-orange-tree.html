Cranford: the quiet art of making a room laugh

Tamsin Greig leads an ensemble in Richmond, where Elizabeth Gaskell’s village becomes a study in comic timing and private grief.

Acting UK editorial 16th November 2026

A village in which the smallest social transgression can furnish a whole afternoon’s conversation is rather good territory for actors. Elizabeth Gaskell’s Cranford has arrived at the Orange Tree Theatre in an adaptation by Lin Coghlan and Shaun McKenna, co-created and directed by Josh Roche. Tamsin Greig plays Matty Jenkyns.

The Guardian’s review on 15th November admires Greig’s balance of comedy and sorrow. Jonathan Evans, writing for The Spy in the Stalls on 13th November, also singles out her portrayal of unfulfilled desire and notes Abigail Cruttenden’s versatility in contrasting roles. Evans finds the closing sentiment a little emphatic, even as he welcomes the production’s warmth.

For actors, the interest lies in the difference between playing a social convention and playing the person who depends on it. A character may insist upon propriety while desperately needing companionship. In a room where spectators surround the action, a glance or a hesitation can be as revealing as a speech. The ensemble must keep the village alive beyond whichever character happens to be speaking.

The run continues until 16th January 2027. The theatre offers £20 tickets for under-thirties, excluding its highest-priced seats, and half-price tickets for eligible theatre union and guild members, excluding half-price previews.
