Dogstar: a stranded crew, a restless new playwright and divided critics

Daisy Hall’s Arctic-set dark comedy at Hampstead Downstairs has drawn contrasting responses, with praise for its three performers.

Acting UK editorial · 13 November 2026

Daisy Hall has returned to Hampstead’s Downstairs studio after Bellringers, this time putting three whalers on a ship trapped in Arctic ice. The crew are running out of strength, the captain is out of sight and the men can no longer agree on what they are seeing. Lucy Morrison directs, with Justin Edwards, Abdul Jalloh and Adam Rojko Vega.

The critical response is interestingly divided. Cheryl Markosky, writing for London Theatre, found the collision of humour and the uncanny rewarding. Lucy Popescu in the Camden New Journal admired the ensemble and the intimacy of the staging, although she felt Hall’s second play needed further development.

The Guardian was less persuaded, finding the dramatic tension insufficient to sustain the long wait for rescue. The differences between these notices suggest that the play’s deliberately static situation is either its central fascination or its principal limitation, depending on the critic.

For actors, a three-hander in a small studio offers a useful opportunity to think about status, listening and the way humour can survive inside a threatening situation. The three performers have to make the relationships carry a great deal of dramatic weight, something worth considering alongside the disagreements about the script.
