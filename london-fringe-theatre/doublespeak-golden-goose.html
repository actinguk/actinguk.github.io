Doublespeak: the classics have a few things to say about spin

A new early modern theatre festival in Camberwell gives emerging performers a chance to examine the politics of language.

Acting UK editorial 15th November 2026

The early modern stage was hardly innocent of political spin. Its characters lie, flatter, bargain and occasionally discover that a well-turned sentence can be more dangerous than a sword. Doublespeak, a two-night festival at the Golden Goose Theatre in Camberwell, takes that observation as its starting point.

The inaugural event from 13th Night Theatre Company brings together scenes from early modern drama on 18th and 19th November. The company’s official booking information describes an approach centred on emerging artists and the ways language can conceal or expose intentions. Rather than presenting a single complete play, the festival offers a collection of scenes from the period.

For performers, the format has an obvious attraction. Working on a short scene can bring the mechanics of classical dialogue into sharp focus: who holds the power, what is being withheld, and what changes when a character chooses a particular word? A speech that looks formidable on the page may become considerably more manageable once its immediate objective is clear. The historical distance need not mean emotional distance.

There is also something welcome about a small theatre making room for early-career artists to test their own approaches to familiar material. Classical work is often discussed as though the principal task were to preserve it. An evening organised around the slipperiness of language suggests a rather different emphasis: discovering what the text can do in the present. The official booking page lists both dates at the Golden Goose; check it for current ticket availability.
