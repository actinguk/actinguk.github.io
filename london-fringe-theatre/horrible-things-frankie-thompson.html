Horrible Things: Frankie Thompson makes discomfort a comic instrument

Two critics consider the clown’s unsettling, inventive solo show, now at Soho Theatre until 21 November.

Acting UK editorial 14 November 2026

What happens when the person commanding the room is also the person most determined to unsettle it? Frankie Thompson’s Horrible Things, directed by Evie Fehilly, puts clowning, recorded voices and audience interaction in the service of a rather unlikely subject: the things people would prefer not to remember.

Andrzej Lukowski’s five-star Time Out notice, based on the Edinburgh Fringe production, singled out a shift in Thompson’s performing style. Where earlier shows had relied heavily on lip-sync and audiovisual collage, he found a more assertive live persona at the centre of this one. Christiana Rose, reviewing the London run for BroadwayWorld on 12 November, also responded positively, particularly to the way the comedy opens onto questions of shame and collective experience.

The two accounts make an interesting pairing for actors. Both recognise that the apparent chaos depends on a performer capable of controlling the room. Clowning does not mean abandoning structure; it means developing enough sensitivity to the audience to make departures from structure feel alive. The use of sound and shifts in tone are equally worth studying.

Horrible Things is at Soho Theatre until 21 November. The venue lists a one-hour running time, an age recommendation of 14+ and prices starting at £13, with individual performance prices varying. This is a roundup of published criticism, not an account of an Acting UK visit.
