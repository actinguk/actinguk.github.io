The Human Voice at the Cockpit puts Elliot Pritchard on the line

A new queer-inflected adaptation of Jean Cocteau’s monologue runs from 24th November to 5th December.

Acting UK editorial22nd November 2026

Jean Cocteau’s The Human Voice arrives at the Cockpit in Marylebone on 24th November for a run through 5th December 2026. Theatre Alive!, in association with Natalie Richardson, presents a physical-theatre adaptation translated and adapted by Jeff Lewis, directed by Andrew Visnevski and performed by Elliot Pritchard as the Woman.

The dramatic arrangement is almost cruel in its simplicity: one person on the telephone, trying to hold on to a lover who is leaving. The audience hears only one side of the conversation. In this production the creative team explicitly connects the work to Cocteau’s queer cultural world, and Pritchard takes the role in his first solo show.

Alexander Mcpherson designs the production, Saro Manoukian composes the music and Natalie Richardson produces. The theatre advertises a seventy-minute performance without an interval, with evening shows from Tuesdays to Saturdays and matinees on 28th November and 5th December.

There is a productive tension in adapting a play built around an absent voice for a physically expressive stage language. How much can the body reveal while the telephone conceals? That is a question for the forthcoming performance, not a verdict we can deliver in advance. The run and creative credits are confirmed by the venue; this is a preview.
