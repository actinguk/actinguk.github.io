A wrong turn on the way home from IKEA leads to Dante’s dark wood

Tania Khan’s I Can See the Sun brings surreal new writing to the Hope Theatre in Islington, with remaining performances through 21 November.

Acting UK editorialPublished 13 November 2026

There’s something wonderfully fringe about the premise of Tania Khan’s I Can See the Sun. A mother and daughter take a wrong turn on their way home from IKEA and find themselves lost on the North York Moors. From there, the ordinary business of getting home gives way to something considerably stranger.

Drawing inspiration from Dante’s Divine Comedy, Khan’s play explores what families pass down from one generation to the next, placing a relationship between mother and daughter inside a surreal, darkly comic landscape.

Khan writes, directs and performs, joined by Karen Cass and Ethan Dillon. Developed with support from ArcolaLAB, the piece comes from theatre company Light the Night.

For actors interested in new writing and work developed outside the conventional commercial theatre circuit, this looks like an intriguing production for the November calendar.
