Lear: Ian McKellen returns to a role that refuses to let him go

A new play at the Yard Theatre brings Shakespeare into conversation with the actor’s own working life, opening on 17 November.

Acting UK editorial 14 November 2026

What happens when a character becomes part of an actor’s biography? Ian McKellen returns to Shakespeare’s ageing monarch next week at the Yard Theatre in Hackney Wick, but the production is not simply another revival of King Lear. Simon Stephens and Jay Miller have made a new play that places the performer’s own reflections alongside the tragedy.

The starting point is an unusual rehearsal archive: conversations recorded during the development of the piece. McKellen plays both the king and an actor, with Taylor Russell as Cordelia. Miller directs. The approach makes the boundary between a fictional loss of authority and a working actor’s relationship with ageing a central question rather than a matter of background research.

For actors, the prospect is particularly interesting. A familiar speech can acquire a different meaning when the audience is invited to consider the person speaking it. How much of a performer’s experience belongs in a character, and when does that experience become material in its own right? This production offers a reason to revisit those questions without treating Shakespeare as a museum piece.

Previews begin on 17 November, with the run continuing to 2 January. Time Out reported in October that seven extra performances had been added. The Yard advises audiences to check its own booking page for newly released seats; its £5 door-ticket scheme for people aged 27 and under applies only when seats remain unsold. A captioned performance is scheduled for 10 December and a BSL-interpreted performance for 16 December.
