Lover Girl: Temi Wilkey takes the solo show into stranger territory

Critical responses differ over the structure of Wilkey’s solo performance, blending stand-up, R&B and theatrical surrealism at Soho Upstairs.

Acting UK editorial14 November 2026

Temi Wilkey’s Lover Girl starts from a recognisable question about modern relationships and takes it somewhere considerably less familiar. Written and performed by Wilkey, with Ragevan Vasan directing, the solo show uses music, direct address and imagined characters to examine desire, identity and self-worth.

The critical responses are far from unanimous. Celia Armand Smith, writing for Fairy Powered Productions, welcomed Wilkey’s energy and the dexterity with which she shifts between comic characterisation and vulnerability. In contrast, Liam O’Dell’s London review on 11 November found the narrative too diffuse, with repetition and abrupt changes of subject undermining the central argument. Andrzej Lukowski’s earlier Edinburgh notice for Time Out took a more mixed position, appreciating Wilkey’s charisma while questioning aspects of the stranger material.

For performers developing their own work, that disagreement is more instructive than a simple star rating. A solo artist has to establish a relationship with the audience and sustain it while changing register, character and sometimes the rules of the performance itself. Material can be playful and autobiographical without dispensing with the need for a clear emotional journey.

Lover Girl continues upstairs at Soho Theatre until 21 November. The venue also lists a workshop with Wilkey on making solo work on 9 December, a particularly relevant follow-up for actors interested in developing their own material.
