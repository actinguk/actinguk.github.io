The Master Builder: ambition begins to look like fear

Greg Hicks stars in David Edgar’s version of Ibsen at the Arcola, where the older artist’s dread of being replaced remains painfully current.

Acting UK · 17th November 2026 · Arcola Theatre, Studio 1

The most revealing thing about an established artist may be what happens when somebody younger enters the room. In Henrik Ibsen’s The Master Builder, the architect Halvard Solness has professional success and an almost paralysing fear of losing it. The combination is not confined to architecture.

At the Arcola Theatre, Greg Hicks plays Solness in David Edgar’s version, directed by Mehmet Ergen. The production runs in Studio 1 until 21st November. According to the venue’s description, the arrival of Hilde Wangel forces the architect to confront a promise from ten years earlier, and the uneasy relationship between his achievements and the damage left behind.

The play offers performers an intricate set of competing impulses. Solness wants admiration but fears succession; Hilde’s challenge appears to awaken both ambition and dread. That contradiction is richer than a straightforward portrait of an ageing tyrant. The difficult work is to make each character’s desires credible, even when their behaviour is far from comfortable.

Ibsen’s dramatic architecture is itself worth studying. The apparently ordinary exchanges carry old debts, half-spoken accusations and shifting power. An actor who rushes to reveal the subtext risks losing the tension that makes the scene work. Sometimes the most consequential line is the one a character has decided not to say.

This is an editorial preview based on the published production information, not an account of attendance or an independent review. The Arcola lists tickets from £15 to £39, although the booking page may show limited availability. The final scheduled performance is 21st November 2026.
