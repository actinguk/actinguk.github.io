NERO puts an emperor’s appetite for performance centre stage

Andrew Barth Feldman leads Steven Sater and Duncan Sheik’s musical at Southwark Playhouse Elephant until 5th December.

Acting UK editorial27th November 2026

Roman emperors are rarely short of theatrical possibilities, but Nero offers a particularly awkward protagonist: an artist who also possesses political power. Southwark Playhouse Elephant is staging a musical about him until 5th December, with Andrew Barth Feldman in the title role.

Steven Sater wrote the book and lyrics, Duncan Sheik the music, and Lindsay Posner directs. The company includes Stephen Carlile as Seneca, Sally Dexter as Agrippina and Nabi Brown as Octavia. Sater and Sheik previously collaborated on Spring Awakening.

The attraction of the premise is the uncomfortable proximity of personal recognition and public authority. A performer can ask for applause; an emperor can demand it. A musical has the means to make that distinction audible, contrasting a character’s self-image with the world occupied by everyone else.

The venue estimates a running time of two hours and thirty minutes, including an interval. A captioned performance is listed for 1st December. The recommended age is fifteen and above; check the venue’s detailed guidance. This is a preview based on published production information.
