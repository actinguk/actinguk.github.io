Relative Motion: when a romance starts obeying different laws

Stephanie Greenwood’s new play uses physics to examine a relationship that moves from attraction towards coercion.

Acting UK editorial14 November 2026

Two strangers meet on a train, and for a while it looks as though they are travelling towards the same destination. In Stephanie Greenwood’s Relative Motion, the image becomes a way of thinking about a relationship whose direction is not quite what one of the people inside it believes.

The play follows physics student Sam and aspiring novelist Paul, played by Greenwood and Sean Hart, with Wiebke Green directing. Sam already has plenty to contend with outside the relationship, including a family member’s illness and her academic ambitions. The initial romance gradually gives way to a much darker examination of power and trust.

For actors, the premise raises a useful question about playing a relationship from inside it rather than from the perspective of someone who already knows how the story ends. A scene may register as affectionate to one character and controlling to the audience. The challenge is to let both readings exist without signalling the outcome in advance.

Relative Motion began its run on 11 November at Southwark Playhouse Borough’s Little theatre. The press-night calendar lists 17 November, so this article is a preview rather than a critical assessment. Performances continue until 5 December; the venue lists a 90-minute running time and a captioned performance on 26 November at 7.30pm. Check the venue for current tickets and its guidance concerning depictions of relationship violence.
