Roleplay: when the personal brand becomes the part

Hannah Reilly’s solo show reaches its final week at Soho Theatre, with critics admiring the performer more consistently than the play.

Acting UK editorial 16th November 2026

An actor is accustomed to being asked to sell a version of themselves. For Danni, the struggling podcaster at the centre of Hannah Reilly’s Roleplay, the version begins to take over the original. Written and performed by Reilly and directed by Paige Rattray, the solo play has transferred from Edinburgh to Soho Theatre.

The London notices offer an interesting distinction between the performer and her material. Cindy Marcolina, writing for BroadwayWorld on 27th October, finds Reilly engaging and her comic delivery sharp, but considers the narrative predictable and its treatment of sexual politics insufficiently developed. Keith Mckenna in British Theatre Guide likewise finds the performance entertaining and its critique of commercial exploitation intelligible, while questioning the depth of its various strands.

There is a useful lesson here for anyone developing a solo show. A performer’s charisma can establish an immediate relationship with the audience, but it cannot necessarily supply the dramatic consequences that a script has left unwritten. Reilly’s premise also invites a more specific question about character: how does someone behave when the persona they have invented starts earning more approval than the person underneath?

The production runs until 21st November, with a seventy-minute running time and tickets from £20, subject to demand. It is strictly for audiences aged eighteen and over; the venue provides content guidance.
