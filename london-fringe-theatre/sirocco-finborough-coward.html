Sirocco: the Noël Coward play that refused to behave

The Finborough revisits Coward’s notorious early failure, restoring censored material and asking whether a scandal can outlive its original audience.

Acting UK · 17th November 2026 · Finborough Theatre

A notorious first night is not necessarily the same thing as a bad play. Noël Coward’s Sirocco has carried the reputation of its disastrous 1927 premiere for almost a century, an evening remembered for angry spectators and a young dramatist whose appetite for provocation had apparently exceeded the public’s patience.

At the Finborough Theatre, Brigid Larmour has returned to the text with a rather more interesting question than whether the original audience was right. The theatre says her research uncovered material removed before the premiere for fear of the censor. This new version restores that writing and offers the play its first London production in ninety-nine years.

Set among English expatriates in Italy, the story follows Lucy Griffin, whose respectable marriage begins to look less attractive when she encounters the unconventional Sirio Marson. Her escape towards an imagined freedom proves more complicated than the fantasy. The premise suggests a Coward interested not simply in drawing-room wit but in the social cost of a woman choosing her own life.

For actors, the revival presents an unusually useful problem: how to play the manners of an earlier age without treating the people inside them as museum exhibits. A character may be constrained by convention and still be fiercely alive. Comedy, after all, often begins where someone refuses to behave as expected.

This is a preview, not a review: the production has not yet opened. Sirocco runs from 24th November to 22nd December 2026. Preview tickets on 24th and 25th November are listed at £18; later prices rise according to the performance week.
