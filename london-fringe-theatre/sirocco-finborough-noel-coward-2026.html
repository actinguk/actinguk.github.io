Noël Coward’s Sirocco returns to London after 99 years at the Finborough

Brigid Larmour’s new version opens at the Finborough Theatre on 24th November and runs until 22nd December.

Acting UK editorial22nd November 2026

A Noël Coward play with one of the more notorious opening-night reputations in British theatre is returning to London. The Finborough Theatre presents Sirocco in a new version edited and directed by Brigid Larmour, from 24th November to 22nd December 2026. The venue bills it as the first London production for ninety-nine years.

Set in 1920s Italy, the story follows Lucy Griffin, an Englishwoman on holiday with her older husband Stephen and his mother. When Stephen departs on business, Lucy is drawn towards the unconventional Sirio Marson. The promise of escape leads her towards an artist’s studio in Florence, but the romance proves less simple than the imagined liberation.

The Finborough’s account recalls an exceptionally hostile response to the play’s original reception, including disorder after the curtain. Such theatrical folklore is entertaining, but it is also a poor substitute for looking afresh at a text. A small auditorium offers a particularly exposed setting in which to test whether Coward’s portrait of desire and respectability has changed with the times.

The first two performances, on 24th and 25th November, are listed as previews. The venue schedules evening performances through the run and selected matinees; there are no Sunday performances in the first week. This is a preview based on the theatre’s published information, not a review of a production we have attended.
