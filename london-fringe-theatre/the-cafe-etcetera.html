The Café: a little shelter in a city that rarely sits still

Roccia Zhang’s multilingual play brings four London lives together at the Etcetera Theatre during Voila! Theatre Festival.

Acting UK editorial 15th November 2026

A café is one of the few places in London where a person can rent a little warmth for the price of a drink. Roccia Zhang’s new play The Café, appearing at the Etcetera Theatre on 17th November as part of Voila! Theatre Festival, uses that modest transaction as the setting for a story about belonging.

Four people meet in a city where their circumstances are becoming increasingly precarious. Money, immigration status and uncertainty about the future sit alongside the ordinary business of conversation. The production is directed by Siyao Wu, with Gus Teodosio, Max Torin, Nana Kim and Parnian Tasaodi in the cast. The venue lists English, Farsi, Korean and Portuguese among its languages.

For actors, multilingual theatre offers an interesting reminder that understanding a scene is not always the same as understanding every word. Gesture, hesitation and the way a performer receives a line can carry information that a translation alone cannot supply. In a story about people finding temporary refuge in one another’s company, those small exchanges may be particularly significant. The premise invites attention to the ensemble rather than to any single character’s eloquence.

The production lasts an hour, with two performances on 17th November, at one and five in the afternoon. Tickets are listed at £12. The venue’s programme notes references to displacement, distress and swearing. This is a preview based on the theatre’s published information; the production has not been reviewed here.
