The Moth: can two men ever escape the history that binds them?

Paul Herzberg’s London premiere at the Finborough has inspired contrasting critical reactions to its exploration of apartheid and forgiveness.

Acting UK editorial · 13 November 2026

A railway encounter between a Black British journalist and a former white South African soldier provides the starting point for Paul Herzberg’s The Moth. The consequences of that meeting unfold over decades, bringing the pair back into each other’s lives until they face the public together. The play arrives at the Finborough after a northern tour in 2025.

Jake Murray directs Faz Singhateh and Micky Cochrane, returning to roles they developed during that earlier run. Their characters are tied together by the aftermath of apartheid, but they have very different reasons for wanting the relationship to continue.

The published reviews offer strikingly different accounts of the evening. Simon Jenner at FringeReview was particularly impressed by the emotional revelations and the contrasting qualities of the performances. John Groves, writing for LondonTheatre1, felt the script lingered too long over its arguments, even while acknowledging the actors’ commitment to the material.

For performers, this is a useful case study in sustaining a relationship across multiple periods of a character’s life. The production asks its leads to convey a changing balance of power without losing the accumulated history between them. The debate over the writing makes that work all the more interesting to consider.
