Wits End: a funeral with rather too much unfinished business

Estée Stimler’s family comedy puts grief and obligation in a room of fifty-five seats.

Acting UK editorial 16th November 2026

There are few occasions on which a family is less inclined to behave itself than a funeral. Estée Stimler’s Wits End brings three siblings together to bury their father, Marvin Becker. One has provided the care, another the money; the third has plenty to say about both. The rabbi, hired online, turns out to have a second career in comedy.

Steve Furst directs the play’s first run at the Circle & Star Theatre in Hampstead. Heather Jeffery, reviewing for London Pub Theatres on 12th November, awards four stars and particularly enjoys Sue Kelvin’s interfering Aunt Phyllis. Jeffery appreciates the use of the small stage and the ensemble’s commitment, although she would welcome more variation in the speed of the dialogue.

Farce is often mistaken for a matter of acceleration, when its most useful resource can be the sudden absence of noise. A silence after an ill-judged remark allows the audience to register the damage before the next character rushes to repair it. Here the spectators are cast as the funeral congregation, making the management of that shared silence an especially interesting acting problem.

The run ends on 22nd November, with the final performance at three in the afternoon. Tickets are £25; consult the official booking page for availability.
