An agent meeting is a conversation, not a coronation

What actors can usefully ask about representation, communication and expectations before agreeing to work together.

Acting UK · 21st November 2026

An invitation to meet an agent can feel like a verdict on a career. It is not. Representation is a working relationship, and both parties need to understand what the other can offer. A good meeting is not simply a test of whether an actor can appear agreeable for half an hour.

Actors should be ready to explain the work they want to pursue and the material that currently represents them. Specific ambitions are more useful than a promise to do absolutely anything. The latter may sound flexible, but it gives an agent very little information.

Ask how submissions and auditions are usually communicated, what updates the agency expects, and how quickly availability needs to be confirmed. It is also reasonable to discuss the kinds of projects the agent sees as realistic starting points. None of these questions requires an interrogation lamp.

Contracts deserve a separate, careful reading. Commission arrangements, exclusivity, notice periods and the scope of representation should be understood before signing. Where wording is unclear, seek qualified advice rather than relying on a friendly verbal summary.

A meeting can also reveal differences in working style. One agent may prefer frequent updates; another may want contact only when there is substantive news. Neither approach is automatically wrong, but an actor who needs one and receives the other may become frustrated.

We would judge the conversation by its clarity, not by the number of compliments exchanged. Representation works best when enthusiasm is accompanied by expectations that can actually be met.
