An acting CV should make the work easy to find

A practical look at credits, training and why a clear acting CV beats an impressive-looking inventory.

Acting UK · 20th November 2026

An acting CV is not an autobiography, and it is certainly not a competition to fill the page. Its immediate purpose is to help a casting professional understand what an actor has done, where they trained and what they can credibly offer a production.

We favour a structure that can be read quickly. Put the relevant professional information where the reader expects it, separate screen and stage work where useful, and identify the production, role and director or company accurately. A credit that requires detective work is doing its owner no favours.

Early-career performers face an awkward problem: a short list can look modest beside the credits of someone who has worked for decades. Padding the page with vaguely described projects is not a solution. Training productions and short films can be valuable evidence of practice, provided they are labelled honestly.

Skills deserve the same discipline. A language, accent, instrument or physical ability may be useful to casting, but only if the actor can demonstrate it at the level claimed. There is a substantial difference between conversational French and a role that demands fluent dialogue. Precision protects everyone from an embarrassing afternoon.

An actor's CV also needs to agree with their other materials. If the showreel, profile and CV use different role names or imply different credits, the reader is left to reconcile them. Consistency is a modest form of professionalism that costs nothing.

Formatting should support reading rather than draw attention to itself. Clear headings, sensible spacing and an accessible file are more useful than ornate typography. A casting team may view the document on a phone, forward it or print it. The design should survive all three.

We would review the document after each significant job, not only when an audition deadline arrives. Remove outdated claims, correct contact information and check that links still work. An accurate, restrained CV makes a stronger argument than a crowded one.

The aim is not to make a career appear longer. It is to make the career that exists intelligible.
