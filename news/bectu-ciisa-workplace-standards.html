Bectu renews its call for safer creative workplaces

The union is urging employers to implement the Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority’s framework, with particular attention to reporting concerns.

Acting UK editorial14 November 2026

Bectu has renewed its call for creative-industry employers to put independent workplace standards into practice. In a statement on 12 November, the union’s head, Philippa Childs, argued that existing internal reporting arrangements are not adequate and that people should be able to raise concerns without fearing damage to their careers.

The framework at the centre of the call comes from the Creative Industries Independent Standards Authority (CIISA). Its published standards cover physical and psychological safety, inclusion, accessible routes for raising problems and a culture that learns from concerns. They are intended to apply across film, television, theatre and music, including smaller organisations and individual productions.

That matters particularly for freelance performers. A short engagement can mean arriving on a new set or in a rehearsal room without knowing the team, the reporting structure or who has authority to intervene. Knowing the production’s reporting arrangements before work starts is a useful practical safeguard. Ask who handles concerns, whether there is an alternative contact outside the immediate line of management, and where the relevant policy can be found.

The Bectu statement is a call for stronger implementation, not an announcement of a new statutory requirement or a guarantee that every production has signed up. Actors can read the framework directly and raise contractual or workplace concerns with their own union or representative.
