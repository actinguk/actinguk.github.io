The BFI backs a more thoughtful approach to screen technology

New funding for NFTS and Falmouth aims to help independent filmmakers work with emerging technology without losing sight of the people involved.

Acting UK editorial18th November 2026

The most useful question about artificial intelligence in film may be neither whether it is coming nor whether it can be stopped. It is who gets to understand it, and on whose terms. On 11th November, the British Film Institute announced support for two projects intended to give independent screen professionals a firmer grasp of emerging technologies.

The National Film and Television School and Falmouth University will each deliver a programme under the BFI National Lottery Innovation Challenge Fund's “In Our Hands” initiative. Over two years, the work will examine how independent filmmakers can use artificial intelligence, virtual production and new capture technologies with greater confidence and appropriate safeguards.

The emphasis is on practical guidance and evidence, rather than simply encouraging producers to adopt whatever arrives next. The BFI says the projects are intended to reduce barriers to experimentation and to support ethical, inclusive approaches to technology. It has not announced an open casting call or a new programme of acting jobs as part of this statement.

For performers, the distinction matters. Virtual production changes the environment in which a scene is made; digital capture can change what happens to a performance afterwards. Understanding those processes is increasingly part of understanding a contract, a rehearsal room and the boundaries of consent. Training for filmmakers is not, by itself, a substitute for negotiated performer protections.

This announcement also arrives as Equity's proposed agreement with Pact puts digital likenesses firmly on the industry's negotiating table. The BFI initiative addresses a different part of the problem: how smaller productions develop the knowledge to make responsible choices before those choices become expensive mistakes.

The programmes' precise training opportunities and eligibility should be checked with the institutions when details are published. For now, this is a funding and research announcement, not an invitation to audition. The BFI is the sole primary source for the details reported here.
