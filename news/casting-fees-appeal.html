Spotlight fees are heading back to court. What does it mean for actors?

Equity says its appeal over Spotlight’s legal status will be heard on 24 November. The outcome is not yet known.

Acting UK editorialPublished 13 November 2026

What this means for actors

Equity’s long-running challenge to the cost of accessing acting work reaches its next stage on 24 November, when the Court of Appeal is scheduled to hear the union’s case concerning Spotlight.

At the heart of the dispute is a familiar frustration: paying to access a casting platform before securing work through it. Equity argues Spotlight should be subject to the regulations governing employment agencies. The High Court rejected its claim in September 2025; the union subsequently secured permission to appeal.

The outcome may have implications beyond Spotlight. Separately, Equity is campaigning to change the rules that permit upfront casting directory fees. For now, the hearing is not a ruling, and actors should not assume their membership fees or access terms have changed.

Equity is inviting performers to share their experiences of casting platforms through its survey and to contact their MPs. Follow the union’s original update for the next steps.
