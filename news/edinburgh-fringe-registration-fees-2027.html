Edinburgh Fringe raises the price of taking a chance

The Fringe Society has announced higher show registration fees for 2027, ending a freeze that began in 2007.

Acting UK editorial 15th November 2026

Taking a show to Edinburgh has never been an especially reliable way to make money. Next year, the arithmetic becomes a little less forgiving. The Edinburgh Festival Fringe Society confirmed on 12th November that its registration charges will rise for the 2027 festival, following a freeze dating back to 2007.

A full run of six or more performances will cost £433 at the standard rate, compared with £393.60 previously. The discounted rate rises from £295.20 to £325. A short run of one or two performances will cost £106, while three to five performances will cost £224. These figures include VAT.

Registration buys a place in the festival’s listings and access to its ticketing infrastructure, not a venue or accommodation. For an actor producing a solo show, the distinction matters: the registration charge is only one line in a budget that may also include rehearsal space, travel, publicity, technical support and a room in Edinburgh during August. The Society offers instalment arrangements for artists who would benefit from spreading the registration payment.

There are changes to the programme itself, too. Shows beginning with numerals will be alphabetised by the written number, ending a small but ingenious way of getting to the front of the listings. The Society is also revising its genre categories and adding more ways to describe a production.

The festival runs from 6th to 30th August 2027. Before committing to a run, performers should compare the registration bands, check venue terms and build a budget that does not depend on selling every seat. Edinburgh can be an excellent place to develop work; it is rather less forgiving of optimistic spreadsheets.
