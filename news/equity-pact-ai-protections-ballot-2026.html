Equity's AI deal gives performers a vote on their digital doubles

A proposed three-year Equity–Pact agreement promises new safeguards for performers’ likenesses, self-tapes and pay. Members now decide whether to accept it.

Acting UK editorial 17th November 2026

An actor's face is not merely another production asset, although artificial intelligence has made that distinction increasingly expensive to defend. On 16th November, Equity announced a proposed three-year agreement with Pact that would introduce collective protections for performers' digital likenesses in UK film and television.

The deal is an offer, not yet a settled agreement. Nearly 8,000 eligible Equity members can vote in an online ballot which opened on 16th November and closes at noon on 30th November. Equity recommends acceptance. The proposed terms would run from January 2027 until 31st December 2029.

According to the union, the package goes well beyond artificial intelligence. It includes a minimum 72-hour notice period for self-tape auditions, excluding weekends and bank holidays; provisions addressing the casting of disabled performers in disabled roles; and contractual expectations around appropriate hair and make-up provision for artists of different ethnicities and cultures. There are also measures concerning intimacy coordination, workplace conduct and environmental practice.

Equity says the offer raises rates by 24 per cent for the biggest cinema productions and by nine per cent for television, alongside changes to other payments. Those figures describe particular parts of a negotiated package, not an across-the-board pay rise for every actor or job.

For working performers, the distinction between consent to appear on camera and consent to have one's likeness reproduced digitally is central. The announcement suggests a framework for negotiating that distinction collectively rather than leaving each individual to confront it in a contract at the end of a long casting process.

The immediate practical question is a democratic one. Eligible members should consult Equity's full ballot material before voting; performers outside the ballot should not assume these proposed terms already apply to their engagements. The union's announcement is the primary source for the offer, and its precise legal effect depends on ratification and the final agreement.
