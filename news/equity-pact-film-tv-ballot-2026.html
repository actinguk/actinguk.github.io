Equity opens ballot on landmark Pact film and television agreement

Nearly 8,000 performers are voting on a proposed three-year deal covering AI replicas, pay and self-tape rules.

Acting UK editorial23rd November 2026

An unusually consequential vote is under way in the British screen industry. Equity opened a ballot on 16th November over a proposed three-year agreement with producers’ body Pact, with almost 8,000 eligible performers invited to decide whether the package should be accepted. Voting closes at noon on 30th November. Equity recommends a yes vote; the result is not yet known.

The union says the proposed agreement would introduce collective safeguards for performers’ digital likenesses, alongside improvements to pay, casting and working conditions. Its headline figures include increases of 24 per cent for rates on the largest films and nine per cent for television across the term of the offer. Those numbers describe proposed changes, not payments already in force.

Self-tape rules are another substantial part of the offer. Equity describes a minimum 72-hour notice period, excluding weekends and bank holidays, a maximum of six pages and no requirement for a reader. In an industry where an audition can quietly consume an entire working day, those are specific limits rather than another polite request to be considerate.

The proposed AI provisions seek to address digital replicas and consent for their use. That does not settle every question about the training of AI systems on existing performances, a gap acknowledged in reporting on the negotiations. It is important not to confuse a negotiated offer with a completed regulatory solution.

The agreement would run from January 2027 to the end of December 2029 if approved. Its significance now lies as much in the ballot as in the text: actors, stunt performers and other eligible members have the final say on the terms under which a large part of British screen production will operate.
