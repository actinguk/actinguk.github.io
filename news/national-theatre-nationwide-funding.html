The National Theatre takes its name seriously

A four-year programme backed by up to £15 million aims to bring theatre and creative training to more than 100,000 young people across England.

Acting UK editorial 15th November 2026

The National Theatre is about to put a little more geography into its name. On 13th November, Culture Secretary Lisa Nandy announced government backing of up to fifteen million pounds for National Theatre Nationwide, a four-year initiative intended to reach more than a hundred thousand young people across England.

The programme brings together twelve regional theatre partners, with performances, workshops and opportunities to explore creative careers. Plans include touring work for four hundred schools, subsidised ten-pound tickets and a music theatre strand in which young people will help devise and perform new work in their own communities. A skills roadshow is intended to reach places without a theatre of their own.

For actors, the interesting part is not simply the audience target. Touring to schools demands a particular kind of performance: the ability to hold a room that may have little familiarity with theatre, often without the comforts of a conventional auditorium. Workshops and participation projects require another set of skills altogether, from listening to improvisation and adapting material to different groups.

The announcement is not a casting call, nor does it specify how many paid performer engagements will result. But actors with experience in theatre for young audiences, facilitation or community performance have good reason to follow the programme and its regional partners as individual projects are announced. The funding is an opportunity to build a wider theatre-going public; the practical question will be how that ambition translates into sustained work for the people who make the theatre.
