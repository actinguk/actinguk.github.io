Sanjeev Bhaskar joins the BFI as archive ambassador

The actor’s appointment on 19th November accompanies a preservation project for more than 10,000 hours of breakfast television.

Acting UK editorial26th November 2026

The BFI announced Sanjeev Bhaskar as its new ambassador on 19th November, with a remit to support fundraising and draw attention to the National Archive’s preservation work. His first engagement was scheduled for 24th November at BFI Southbank, revisiting the history of TV-am.

The reunion was advertised with presenters including Lorraine Kelly, Anne Diamond, Nick Owen, Anneka Rice and Timmy Mallett. It accompanies an archive project intended to preserve the broadcaster’s collection of more than 10,000 hours from 1983 to 1992, currently stored on vulnerable video formats.

Bhaskar’s career has encompassed the sketch comedy Goodness Gracious Me, the sitcom The Kumars at No. 42 and the ITV crime series Unforgotten. The BFI’s announcement also identifies his recent work on The Split Up. His new position is advocacy for screen heritage, rather than a production or acting appointment.

For performers, an archive has a particular value: it records work that may no longer be available through the commercial services that originally distributed it. The announcement makes no promise of immediate public access to the whole TV-am collection. Digitisation and preservation are the announced priorities.
